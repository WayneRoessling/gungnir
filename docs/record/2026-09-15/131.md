**The decision walk of 2026-09-15: D-44 to D-52, and what it freed.** Item 130
cleared the signatures; this is the other half of the same pass. Every numbered
decision on the ledger, D-01 to D-43, was already Resolved, so what was actually
blocking work was a set of questions nobody had filed a D-number for -- each one
recorded inside the entry that tripped over it, where a reader of the register's own
summary table could not see it. Nine were walked with the owner and taken; four new
gaps came out of them.

**D-44 descopes STANAG 4676, and that is the largest single unblocking.** GAP-064's
rule is a pinned edition and a public sample before any codec, and
`docs/design/external-standards.md` §2 records that neither is obtainable: the cover
agreement is listed with its edition unverified, AEDP-12 is the implementation
document, and no download link for either could be confirmed. So the rule was
unsatisfiable rather than unfunded. 4676 leaves the release with `Stanag4676Codec`
still refusing by name; **GAP-064 and GAP-063 both close**, the latter because its own
action called the 4676 row the whole of what was left of the interface conformance
suite; and GAP-001 loses its last dependency. Recorded as an amendment to D-01's scope
lock, the way D-33 recorded an addition to it -- a descope taken deliberately and
written down, not a capability allowed to lapse. Writing the codec from secondary
descriptions was refused by name: that is the written-from-memory outcome GAP-064
exists to prevent, and a conformance suite gated on a guessed wire format would be
precisely the false claim this workspace forbids. The way back in is recorded on the
decision.

**D-45 gives three entries the rule they stopped at.** A laydown's first-engagement
range per approach is the worst case -- the minimum over the run -- labelled as such
and carrying the count of predictions behind it. The argument is US-15's own table:
it compares two laydowns on that column, and an averaged figure flatters the weaker
one. GAP-020, GAP-045 and GAP-087 all read from it.

**D-50 took the owner's answer over the recommendation, and it is the bigger scope.**
All three of GAP-045's undesigned controls are filed rather than the one that was
recommended: GAP-105 (a rehearsal cannot move the sensors a laydown declares),
GAP-106 (an operator cannot accept a coverage gap) and GAP-107 (nothing gates a plan
on having been rehearsed). Two of the three have no design at all, so each gets a note
before code. GAP-105 is the one that matters first, and its own entry records why:
round 1 had to declare a third laydown, with a radar forward-sited 10 km up the
approach, purely to manufacture a coverage difference the test could measure -- which
is scenario content standing in for a missing capability.

**The rest.** D-46 keeps PHD as the dense-group default and makes CPHD selectable
through the `TrackingConfig` vocabulary GAP-015 already owed, so nobody's shipped
behaviour changes underneath them. D-47 gives a node **one persisted TLS identity for
both roles**, which needs the `gungnir-security` generalisation item 105 named. D-48
records the Azure key version in the wrapped-secret file, chosen over a documented
rotation procedure because the failure that procedure would guard against is a node
unable to open its own keystore after a routine rotation. D-49 retires GAP-082's
central differential harness and re-points `docs/agentic-workflow.md` §1 at the
distributed suite that actually runs. D-51 keeps `proj` opt-in and files the vertical
datum as GAP-108. D-52 confirms that a cooperatively-identified UAS gets no platform
class -- Category 129 carries a registration authority's strings, not a capability
category -- so GAP-080's classifier still owns that question.

**Three statuses moved that no decision touched.** GAP-004 was still asking for a
signature that landed 2026-09-08 (item 106); GAP-057's own action recorded its
2026-09-10 signature while the status never followed; GAP-100's three remaining items
were each done or owned elsewhere. **This is the same failure items 123, 125, 126 and
130 each record, found a fifth time by the same method** -- reading every entry's own
text against the signature record rather than trusting the status column. The register
is the document this project uses to answer "what is next", and a status column that
lags its own prose is how a closed gap goes on looking like work.

**Two things found while preparing the owner's own steps, and fixed rather than
handed over broken.** `gpu-fusion.yml`'s header still said the `gpu-tests` feature was
empty and would run zero tests; GAP-024 has since written four `#[ignore]`d
`#[tokio::test]`s behind it, and `cargo test -p gungnir-data-fusion --features
gpu-tests --no-run` compiles them, so the comment was a year-old-sounding claim about
a state that had changed under it. And `release.yml`'s `workflow_dispatch` ran every
job: the assurance half GAP-061 item (2) calls "the half that can be rehearsed without
publishing anything" was followed by `build` and then by `sign`, which uses keyless
cosign and records every signature in Sigstore's **public, append-only transparency
log**. A rehearsal would have published. The dispatch now takes an `assurance_only`
input defaulting to true, and `build`, `sign` and `container` are guarded on it; a tag
push is unaffected and still runs the whole workflow.
